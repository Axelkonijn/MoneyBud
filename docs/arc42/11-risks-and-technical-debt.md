# 11. Risks and Technical Debt

**What belongs here:** Known technical risks and accumulated debt, stated plainly, ideally with
what could be done about each.

This section is only worth anything if it is honest. A risk section listing no real risks tells the
reader the document isn't maintained.

---

| Risk / debt | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Manual entry and accurate net worth pull against each other.** An accurate net worth requires every transaction to be recorded; requiring that is exactly the friction that makes people abandon budgeting apps. The app cannot detect a forgotten transaction, because nothing outside it knows what the truth is | High — this is the most likely cause of the product quietly becoming useless | Not solved. Options include letting balances be corrected by hand, or reconciling entered balances against calculated ones and reporting the difference. Bank import would largely remove it, but conflicts with local operation |
| **Two mechanisms will write the same account balance.** Once accounts exist, an *account-backed* category ([§12](12-glossary.md)) makes a balance move as a **side effect of a planning action**: assigning to Savings transfers real money into its backing account, and the end-of-period sweep does the same unprompted. Both movements now have a designated **source** as well — the *pool account* ([§12](12-glossary.md)) — so one everyday account is written automatically by every assignment to a backed category and by every sweep, which makes it the single balance most exposed to this. Meanwhile round 2 settles that the user can also edit a balance freely by hand. So a balance is authored from two directions — one automatic, one manual — and, as the row above records, nothing outside MoneyBud knows which is right. The failure is quiet: the user tops up savings in the bank app, MoneyBud transfers the same money again on assignment, and the balance is double-counted with nothing to contradict it. **A third route reaches a wrong balance from the other side.** An expense whose category is unbacked now *defaults* to the pool account rather than asking ([§12](12-glossary.md)), so an expense paid in **cash** and entered without overriding the account is deducted from the wrong place: the pool account reads low by money that never left it and the cash account reads high by money that did, and nothing outside MoneyBud will say so. Cash is the likely case precisely because nothing about it looks different at entry. This one is **known and accepted** — it was argued out before the default was taken, the override exists, and the decision saved on every expense was judged worth it ([§12](12-glossary.md), quality goal 2) — so it belongs here as a recognised cost, not as an unnoticed hole | Medium, and **latent** — there are still no accounts in the code, so nothing can go wrong yet. Becomes high the moment backing is built, because a wrong balance corrupts net worth, which is one of the two questions the app exists to answer ([§1.1](01-introduction-and-goals.md)). A hand edit also makes the two halves of the model disagree openly: *Accumulated* ([§12](12-glossary.md)) is computed purely from assignments and expenses, so it does not follow an edited backing account, and because backing is many-to-many the difference cannot be attributed to any one category | Not solved, and deliberately not solved now. Decide it **before** accounts are built rather than after: this is exactly the kind of thing that is expensive to discover late, because the choice shapes how balances are stored. The options to weigh are whether a hand-edit is an overwrite or an adjustment entry, whether a balance is stored or derived from its transactions, and whether MoneyBud shows entered and calculated balances side by side rather than picking one. The expense default adds no separate mitigation and needs none: if balances end up **derived from their transactions**, a mis-defaulted account is one entry to re-point rather than a balance to unpick, which is a point in that option's favour. Making the account easy to change after the fact matters more here than any warning at entry would, since the whole point of the default is that nothing at entry knows it is wrong |
| **Single stakeholder, who is also the only user and only developer.** No external party will notice if the product solves the wrong problem | Medium — a wrong direction can run a long way before anything contradicts it | Stakeholder input is recorded verbatim in `docs/stakeholder/` so that later reasoning can be checked against what was actually said, not against what was remembered |
| **Money representation is settled in code and on disk, and its stored form is the part that will be expensive to change** (see [§8.2](08-crosscutting-concepts.md)). Settled: amounts are always a whole number of cents, refused at entry if finer, so nothing in MoneyBud ever rounds; in code an amount is a signed `Money` value type over a `long` of cents, transaction amounts are positive magnitudes with direction carried by the transaction type, and there is no currency field ([ADR 0003](../decisions/0003-money-representation.md)). **Since the persistence increment, how amounts are stored is settled and built too**: whole cents as JSON integers, read strictly, so a fraction of a cent or cents written as text make the file unreadable rather than rounded ([ADR 0007](../decisions/0007-keeping-the-ledger.md)). What remains is **the standing condition on the whole-cents rule**: it holds only while every amount is typed in rather than computed, and §8.2 lists what would reopen it — a split leftover, a percentage, interest, a bank import, a second currency. It now reaches the file as well, which has nowhere to put a fraction of a cent | Low **while the data is demo data**: the stakeholder ruled that a new version need not read an older one's demo data ([§12](12-glossary.md), *Demo data may not survive a new version*), so a wrong format costs a fresh start, not a migration. The format is version 1 and reads nothing older. It rises to medium at the **switch to real use**, from which data must be carried across versions: a stored representation is the part that is genuinely expensive to change once real data exists. The stakeholder has ruled that his saves may be dropped at least up to and including the accounts increment ([§12](12-glossary.md), *Real use before accounts*), so nothing before then has to plan around it | The code half and the storage half are decided and built. **The file names its format and version**, so a later format is met by "cannot read" rather than misread, and reading an older version can start there when the switch to real use makes it a requirement. Treat the standing condition as a live rule rather than history: anyone adding a feature that *computes* an amount reopens §8.2 before writing it, and `Money` has no division in order to make that hard to do by accident |
| **The Desktop has no automated tests, by plan.** `MoneyBud.Desktop` is the Avalonia window and `RingControl`. No scenario and no unit test starts it ([ADR 0006](../decisions/0006-three-source-projects.md)). It was checked by running it on Windows, and by rendering it headless with synthetic data outside the repository ([ADR 0005](../decisions/0005-avalonia-ui-toolkit.md)). That is sound only while it decides nothing. **It did decide one thing, and that was moved out:** as first built, narrowing the suggestions was Avalonia's own *Contains* filter, set in the window's markup. It is now `MoneyBudApp.SuggestionMatches` in the presentation layer, which the Desktop only hands to the control (*Resolved*, below). The one piece of behaviour left in the Desktop is its once-a-minute timer, which since the persistence increment calls `MoneyBudApp.Tick` rather than only refreshing ([§8.4](08-crosscutting-concepts.md)). **The first demo put three rulings next to that line** (2026-09-26, [§12](12-glossary.md)), and the build kept two of them out of the Desktop. The minimum slice width is `Ring.MinimumSweep` in the presentation layer, and `RingControl`'s own hidden minimum was removed. What pointing shows is decided by `MoneyBudApp` and `Ring.SliceAt`, and the Desktop only turns the pointer's position into a share. The third, the order of a form's fields, can live only in the window's markup. **It is the one exception to "no automated tests"**: `WindowMarkupTests` reads the markup as text, approved at the plan gate as a small departure from ADR 0006 ([§8.4](08-crosscutting-concepts.md), *Tests that read the window's markup*). **The corrections increment added a good deal of wiring and no decisions.** Which buttons a category row shows comes from `CategoryRow.CanDelete`, `IsRenaming` and `IsArchived`, whether a form is in *Wijzigen* from the form's own state, and whether the question is showing from `MoneyBudApp`. The Desktop binds to each. Its one new behaviour is a side effect, not a choice: the minute's refresh rebuilds an open rename box, which loses focus ([§8.4](08-crosscutting-concepts.md)). **The persistence increment gave the Desktop wiring and one small window, and still no decisions.** It makes a `FileLedgerStore` on the default folder, hands it to `MoneyBudStart.Start`, and shows the main window or a message window whose text is `StartResult.Refused.Text`. Its timer now calls `MoneyBudApp.Tick`, and the window's `Closed` event calls `Close`. Whether MoneyBud starts, what it says, when it saves, retries and gives up are all in the presentation layer. The save line is bound to `SaveLine`, and its colour to `IsUnsaved`. **A second markup test** holds that the save line is a sibling of the notice and the question, so it cannot drift into their place unnoticed, which widens the one exception on the same terms ([§8.4](08-crosscutting-concepts.md), *Tests that read the window's markup*). The message window itself is built in code, not markup, and no test sees it | Low. Everything the screen *decides* is in the presentation layer and is run by the scenarios or unit tests, except the field order and the save line's place, which the markup tests hold. **One piece of wiring now matters more than any drawing:** if the Desktop ever stopped calling `Close` on the window's `Closed` event, or `Tick` on its timer, the last save attempt or the retry would silently stop, and every test would stay green. The headless run of the real window on 2026-09-26 saw a retry succeed, and nothing automated holds either call. What can go wrong here unnoticed is what is *drawn*: a slice in the wrong place, a marker bound to the wrong flag, a button that calls the wrong command. **The corrections increment made the last of these likelier**: the window now has four kinds of button labelled *Verwijderen*, on each entry form, in the question and on a category row, bound to four different commands, and a swapped binding would pass every test. **Compiled bindings** catch a binding to something that does not exist at build time, but not a binding to the wrong thing that does. Rises if logic starts collecting in the window, because that logic would be green by default | Keep "deliberately thin" a **rule**: a decision found in the Desktop moves to `MoneyBud.Presentation`, where the scenarios reach it. Check the window by running it after any change to what the Overview shows. If the drawing ever needs guarding, Avalonia's headless mode is the route to an automated check. It has been used twice outside the repository, the second time to chase the "groc" defect after the first demo ([§12](12-glossary.md)). It was left out deliberately, not overlooked |
| **A settled rule the code cannot honour yet: the period start day cannot change once budgets exist.** [§12](12-glossary.md) says the start day is configurable. `Ledger` takes its `BudgetPeriodCalendar` at construction and has no way to change it, and budgets are stored keyed by category and the **first day** of their period. Changing the start day after any budget exists would leave every budget attached to a period that no longer exists. Expenses and income carry their own dates and would re-sort by themselves; budgets would not. This is why the UI increment keeps the start day at the 1st ([§12](12-glossary.md), *The period start day stays at the 1st, for now*). **Since the persistence increment the kept file has the same shape**: each budget is written against its period's first day, and the start day itself is not written ([ADR 0007](../decisions/0007-keeping-the-ledger.md)). Loading refuses a budget on a day that starts no period, so a start day changed in code would make an existing file **unreadable**, not silently misread | Low today, though no longer for the reason it used to be. Until the persistence increment nothing was kept, so the start day could be chosen before any budget existed without harm. **Keeping data is now built** (2026-09-26, [§8.3](08-crosscutting-concepts.md)), so there is always a budget in the way once one has been assigned. While the kept data is still demo data it stays low all the same: deleting the file and starting fresh is an accepted way out ([§12](12-glossary.md), *One set of data*, *Demo data may not survive a new version*). That way out stays open at least up to and including the accounts increment, because the stakeholder does not mind his saves being dropped until then ([§12](12-glossary.md), *Real use before accounts*). Only at the switch to real use does it close, and this row rise | Not solved, and not solvable by code alone. First **decide** what happens to existing budgets when the start day changes, and put it to the stakeholder. Then store or migrate budgets to match. Nothing about it is urgent while the UI does not expose the setting, and while starting fresh costs nothing |
| **Desktop-first serves the eventual usage pattern badly.** The deployment form is now decided — desktop, [ADR 0002](../decisions/0002-desktop-application-first.md) — which closes the previous "undecided" risk and replaces it with this one. Expenses are entered while out of the house, so mobile is where entry actually happens, and entry friction is what abandons budgeting apps (quality goal 2). A desktop app is the wrong shape for that | Medium — low while the first version is a throwaway demo, because the demo exists to be reacted to rather than lived in. Rises as soon as the stakeholder starts using it in earnest. **The UI now exists, and entry through it is on the desktop only**, which is where this risk stops being theoretical. **Keeping data does not by itself end the demo** (settled and built 2026-09-26): the stakeholder chose persistence while the data is still demo data, and does not mind his saves being dropped at least up to and including the accounts increment. So this row does not rise with persistence or with opening a period ([§12](12-glossary.md), *Why now: demo now, real soon*, *Real use before accounts*) | Accepted knowingly, with the reasoning in ADR 0002. Mobile and desktop/mobile sync stay deferred, not rejected ([§3.2](03-context-and-scope.md)). Keeping scenarios declarative (`features/README.md`) is what lets the specification survive being pointed at a different UI. **The UI increment kept the way to mobile open on two more fronts:** the toolkit also targets iOS and Android ([ADR 0005](../decisions/0005-avalonia-ui-toolkit.md)), and everything the screen decides sits in a layer with no toolkit that a mobile head could use ([ADR 0006](../decisions/0006-three-source-projects.md)). Neither settles mobile. Reopen ADR 0002 when the demo stops being a demo. **That is the switch to real use, not the persistence increment.** ADR 0002's own wording is "the moment the stakeholder starts keeping data he would mind losing", and demo data kept by the persistence increment is data he has ruled may be thrown away at a version change |
| **The "location" dimension is still not built** — not in the expense increment and not in the income one — and end-of-period money therefore has nowhere real to go. **No transaction in the demo has an account — not an expense, and not an income.** An expense has a category but no account. An income has neither: it names no category by design ([§12](12-glossary.md)) and no account for this reason, so an income record is an amount, a date and a label and nothing else. That makes the gap wider on the income side than on the expense side. Money is recorded as *arriving* with no statement of where it arrived, and the *pool account* — which [§12](12-glossary.md) defines as the place unassigned money is assumed to live — has nothing to be. The knock-on: both *Leftover* and still-*Unassigned* money are supposed to be swept into a chosen destination category at a period end, and [§12](12-glossary.md) now requires that destination to be **account-backed** — precisely so that swept money really lands somewhere. With no accounts there can be no backed category, so there is no valid destination and the sweep cannot run at all; in practice that money vanishes at the boundary | Low, while the data is throwaway, which it still is after persistence is built. Would become high if the demo quietly turns into the real thing — directing what is left over somewhere useful is a core capability ([§1.1](01-introduction-and-goals.md)), and an account is what it needs to point at | Accepted deliberately, and the vanishing money accepted explicitly by the stakeholder on the grounds that the first version is a demo to react to rather than live in ([§1.1](01-introduction-and-goals.md)) and its data is disposable. It stops being acceptable the moment accounts arrive. Revisit then, and in any case before any data is kept that the stakeholder would mind losing. **That second deadline is the switch to real use, not the persistence increment** (2026-09-26): persistence keeps demo data, which the stakeholder has ruled may be thrown away, and the vanishing money was accepted on exactly that ground. **Asked the same day whether trying MoneyBud before accounts exist matters, the stakeholder did not think it relevant**: *"I'll likely try it out a bit before accounts are added but if the saves need to be deleted when accounts get added i dont mind"* ([§12](12-glossary.md), *Real use before accounts*). So the acceptance stands as it was: what he enters before accounts is still throwaway enough to delete. **Income, now built, widened this gap without reopening the decision**: the accountless shape was accepted for the demo as a whole rather than for expenses specifically, and it is the same trade on both sides — an account on an income would need a pool account to be meaningful, and that needs the whole location dimension |
| **All of MoneyBud's data is one file, which MoneyBud never copies.** Built 2026-09-26: `moneybud.json`, in the user's local application data, with backing up left to the user outside MoneyBud, and its location written only in the README, never shown by MoneyBud ([§12](12-glossary.md), *Backing up is the user's business*, *Where the data is, is written in the README*; [§7](07-deployment-view.md)). A damaged file is met by saying so, touching nothing and closing, which protects what is there but recovers nothing. **MoneyBud's own writing is ruled out as a cause of that damage**: an interrupted save, by a crash or a power cut, must leave the previous save intact, losing at most the change being saved ([§12](12-glossary.md), *An interrupted save never damages the previous one*). **Built** as a temporary file, flushed to the disk, then renamed over the data file ([ADR 0007](../decisions/0007-keeping-the-ledger.md)). That removes the likeliest way a single file with no copies loses its whole history. **What holds it is the file system's rename**, and it was checked by unit tests and a scenario that rebuild the state a cut-off save leaves, not by cutting the power during a real save. It does not reach a failing disk, a deleted profile or damage from outside MoneyBud. **A failed save adds a smaller loss**: MoneyBud says so on the save line, keeps it there, retries once a minute and after every change, and makes one last attempt on closing. Closing before any save succeeds loses the changes since, accepted by the stakeholder ([§12](12-glossary.md), *When a save fails, MoneyBud says so and keeps going*). A disk failure, a deleted profile or a damaged file with no backup of the user's own is the whole history gone, and nothing inside MoneyBud reminds anyone that a backup is needed | Low while the data is demo data, which the stakeholder has ruled may be thrown away, at least up to and including the accounts increment. Rises to high once the data is real: the history is the product, and net worth and a period's figures depend on every entry being there | Partly mitigated, and built: an interrupted save cannot damage the previous one, which is the one cause inside MoneyBud's reach. **Backing up is made as easy as the rulings allow**: it is copying one file, which is plain JSON and so can be checked by opening it, and which a save replaces whole rather than rewrites in place ([§7](07-deployment-view.md)). The rest is accepted by the stakeholder's ruling: backing up is not MoneyBud's job. He was offered keeping earlier copies and declined. The mitigation is outside MoneyBud: a backup of the user's own, of a file whose location the README states. If he later wants MoneyBud to help, the declined option is recorded in §12 |

## Resolved

A row leaves the table when its risk is gone. It is listed here so that a reader who remembers it
can see what happened to it.

| Was | Resolved by |
|---|---|
| **The "shown in a period" scenarios were bound to the ledger, not to anything that shows.** The archive scenarios and two assign scenarios asserted that a category "should (not) be shown" in a period, and the steps combined `HasHistoryIn` and `IsArchived`. The archived half of the display rule lived only in the step definitions, and the full rule was settled but implemented nowhere. A green suite could have gone on describing code the user never sees | **The UI increment, 2026-09-25.** The full rule is one domain query, `Ledger.CategoriesShownIn`, and the Overview lists exactly what it returns. The "shown" steps, and "shown as over budget", "shown as over-assigned" and "offered", were rebound to read the presentation layer ([§8.1](08-crosscutting-concepts.md), [§8.4](08-crosscutting-concepts.md)). The in-use half has scenarios of its own in `show-categories-in-a-period.feature` |
| **"2.000" was recorded as €2,00 without a word.** Found by `spec-reviewer` in the UI increment, and resolved before it was ever written into this table. With a point or comma as the decimal mark, "2.000" read as two euros. That is whole cents, so no rule downstream could catch it, while the user very likely meant two thousand. MoneyBud itself shows thousands with a point. And a recorded entry could not then be corrected (below) | **Ruled by the stakeholder on 2026-09-25, after the review, and built.** A mark followed by exactly three digits ending in 0 is refused as ambiguous, and the refusal names both readings ([§12](12-glossary.md), *Typing an amount*). Held by scenarios since 2026-09-26 (next row) |
| **Reading a typed amount had no scenario.** It was ruled at the plan gate and refined after the spec review ([§12](12-glossary.md), *Typing an amount*): comma or point as the decimal mark, no thousands separator, "2.000" refused as ambiguous. It was held only by unit tests (`AmountInputTests`, `FormTests`), and ADR 0004's rule for unit tests says a behaviour asserted only by a unit test means a scenario is missing. A green suite did not show the stakeholder that his ruling held | **`features/type-an-amount.feature`, approved at the scenario gate on 2026-09-26, bound and green**: 14 scenario outlines, 73 cases, across expenses, incomes and assigning. Writing it surfaced two more rulings, taken the same day: four or more whole-cent digits after the mark ("2.0000") are not an amount, and a mark needs a digit on both sides (",50" and "12," are not amounts). Six choices the scenario writer made were approved with it ([§12](12-glossary.md), *Typing an amount*). One of them, that "€ −50" is refused, has no scenario and is held by a unit test instead |
| **The demo could not correct a wrong entry.** The UI increment covered what the domain did, and the domain had no editing or deleting of a transaction ([§12](12-glossary.md), *It covers what the domain does*). A mistyped expense or income stayed for the rest of the run, and because nothing was stored then ([§8.3](08-crosscutting-concepts.md)), the only way to be rid of it was to close MoneyBud and lose everything else too. That sat against the first half of quality goal 3, amounts adjustable without starting over ([§1.2](01-introduction-and-goals.md)). A plan could be corrected, by assigning; a transaction could not. **Accepted by the stakeholder for the demo on 2026-09-25**, and named by him as missing at the first demo, 2026-09-26: *"Maar dat komt later."* It had already shaped one ruling: "2.000" is refused as ambiguous rather than read as €2,00, partly because a wrong amount, once recorded, could not be put right ([§12](12-glossary.md), *Typing an amount*). The row carried two questions for the increment that would fix it: whether an edit is a new record or a rewrite, and what a correction does to a period already swept | **The corrections increment, settled, specified and built on 2026-09-26.** An expense or income can be changed or removed in any period, by clicking its row; removing asks first; a category can be renamed, and one with no history anywhere deleted ([§12](12-glossary.md), *An entry can be changed or removed*). **Both questions are answered.** An edit is a **rewrite**: the entry keeps its id and its place and no record is kept of what it was ([§8.1](08-crosscutting-concepts.md), *An entry has an id*). A correction reaching a swept period either meets the late-expense rule or joins §12's open question about the sweep, which cannot be answered until a sweep exists (*Corrections and the sweep*). What it leaves: a change saved by mistake can be undone only by changing it back from memory, a cost §12 accepted with the rewrite. The "2.000" ruling was not put back to the stakeholder now that one of its reasons has gone, and §12 says why it stands on the others |
| **Narrowing the suggestions lived in the Desktop, held by nothing.** As first built, the category box narrowed with Avalonia's own `AutoCompleteBox` *Contains* filter, set in the window's markup. That put a stakeholder ruling ([§12](12-glossary.md), *Category entry is free text with suggestions*) where no scenario or test reaches, compared by the thread's culture, and able to regress with every test green after a toolkit upgrade | **Moved into the presentation layer on 2026-09-25.** `MoneyBudApp.SuggestionMatches` and `SuggestionsFor` decide it, ordinally with case ignored and runs of whitespace counted as one, and `FormTests` holds it. The Desktop only passes the predicate to the control as a custom filter. The stakeholder did not ask for a scenario for it. This is the first time the Desktop's "decides nothing" rule (the table above) was enforced |
