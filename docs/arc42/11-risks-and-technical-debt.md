# 11. Risks and Technical Debt

**What belongs here:** Known technical risks and accumulated debt, stated plainly, ideally with
what could be done about each.

This section is only worth anything if it is honest. A risk section listing no real risks tells the
reader the document isn't maintained.

---

| Risk / debt | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Manual entry and accurate net worth pull against each other.** An accurate net worth requires every transaction to be recorded; requiring that is exactly the friction that makes people abandon budgeting apps. The app cannot detect a forgotten transaction, because nothing outside it knows what the truth is | High — this is the most likely cause of the product quietly becoming useless | Not solved. Options include letting balances be corrected by hand, or reconciling entered balances against calculated ones and reporting the difference. Bank import would largely remove it, but conflicts with local operation. **The first option was taken on 2026-09-27, and built the same day** ([§12](12-glossary.md), *Accounts and net worth*): a balance is worked out from its entries, and the user puts a wrong one right by typing the real balance, which MoneyBud records as a *balance correction*. Showing entered and calculated balances side by side was rejected. A balance correction takes in every entry dated before it, so a forgotten expense stops showing in the balance. **The second option is recovered for corrected accounts** (ruled and built the same day): the account's history shows each balance correction **with the new balance and the difference**, "Correctie — saldo € 1.000,00 (− € 23,40)". **The difference is recomputed, not fixed**: it is always what is still unexplained. Record the forgotten €23,40 expense, dated before the balance correction, and it shows € 0,00, while the balance stays €1.000. So it is a working reconciliation, not only a trace. As forgotten entries are found it shrinks, and at €0,00 the user knows he has found everything ([§12](12-glossary.md), *The accounts strip, and an account's history*). **What it still does not do**: it says how much is missing, not what. It is shown in the history, not on the Overview, and nothing prompts a look. It exists only for accounts the user corrects. Until the missing entry is found, the purpose side stays short by it, and nothing there reports it. **Recurring entries (settled and built 2026-09-28, [ADR 0011](../decisions/0011-recurring-entries.md)) cut the friction for what repeats, and bring the opposite error** ([§12](12-glossary.md), *Recurring entries*): MoneyBud records each occurrence on its own date whether or not the money moved. A subscription cancelled at the provider, a price changed without the user updating the latest occurrence, a salary paid a day late: each is recorded as scheduled, and nothing outside MoneyBud contradicts it. **What keeps it visible, by ruling**: every recorded occurrence is named once in a notice, the latest one's row carries a grey label, and changing or stopping it is one change in the form. A balance correction then shows the difference like any other. Nothing prompts a check |
| **Two mechanisms will write the same account balance.** Once accounts exist, an *account-backed* category ([§12](12-glossary.md)) makes a balance move as a **side effect of a planning action**: assigning to Savings transfers real money into its backing account, and the end-of-period sweep does the same unprompted. Both movements now have a designated **source** as well — the *pool account* ([§12](12-glossary.md)) — so one everyday account is written automatically by every assignment to a backed category and by every sweep, which makes it the single balance most exposed to this. Meanwhile round 2 settles that the user can also edit a balance freely by hand. So a balance is authored from two directions — one automatic, one manual — and, as the row above records, nothing outside MoneyBud knows which is right. The failure is quiet: the user tops up savings in the bank app, MoneyBud transfers the same money again on assignment, and the balance is double-counted with nothing to contradict it. **A third route reaches a wrong balance from the other side.** An expense whose category is unbacked now *defaults* to the pool account rather than asking ([§12](12-glossary.md)), so an expense paid in **cash** and entered without overriding the account is deducted from the wrong place: the pool account reads low by money that never left it and the cash account reads high by money that did, and nothing outside MoneyBud will say so. Cash is the likely case precisely because nothing about it looks different at entry. This one is **known and accepted** — it was argued out before the default was taken, the override exists, and the decision saved on every expense was judged worth it ([§12](12-glossary.md), quality goal 2) — so it belongs here as a recognised cost, not as an unnoticed hole. **Since 2026-09-27 the hand edit is no longer a free overwrite** (settled, and built the same day): it records a *balance correction*, a dated statement of what the account really held that day, beside the entries ([§12](12-glossary.md), *Accounts and net worth*) | **High since the backing increment was built (2026-09-27)**, as this column said it would become. Medium before that; the text below is kept as written. **Accounts are in the code since 2026-09-27**, so the manual half and the third route are live. The first route, the automatic writer, is still **latent**: nothing moves money by itself yet. Becomes high the moment backing is built, because a wrong balance corrupts net worth, which is one of the two questions the app exists to answer ([§1.1](01-introduction-and-goals.md)). A hand edit also makes the two halves of the model disagree openly: *Accumulated* ([§12](12-glossary.md)) is computed purely from assignments and expenses, so it does not follow an edited backing account, and because backing is many-to-many the difference cannot be attributed to any one category. **The third route is no longer latent**: every expense is on an account, pre-filled with the pool account, so a cash expense left on the default writes a wrong balance. The automatic writer, the first route, stays latent until backing, the increment after. **Backing was settled with the stakeholder on 2026-09-27 and is not built** ([§12](12-glossary.md), *Backing and Accumulated*), so the automatic writer is now designed: an assignment to a backed category moves money from the pool account on the day of assigning or the period's first day if later; backing a category moves its unspent *Remaining* for the current period; and, since the stakeholder's revision the same day, **unbacking moves what is there for the category in its backing account back to the pool account, and re-pointing moves it to the new account**: what MoneyBud moved in for it, minus its expenses paid from that account (made exact in a follow-up). That is four automatic writers, not one. This row rises to high when they are built. **The rulings also make *Accumulated* and a backing account's balance differ on purpose**, which sharpens the "disagree openly" above: by money already in the account when it starts backing (ruled, and accepted with that cost), and by spending against a backed category put on another account (ruled in a follow-up: it lowers *Accumulated* and not the backing account). Because unbacking and re-pointing move what is there for the category in the account, not *Accumulated*, that second difference is **not** left behind: the backing account ends with nothing on the category's behalf, and the pool account exactly down by what it paid. A move **may overdraw** the account it leaves, when that account paid for something else, and that is ruled true: shown *Rood*, never blocked (§12, *Backing and Accumulated*, follow-ups). The amount moved and *Accumulated* can therefore differ, by design. A first version of the rulings let unbacking and backing again move the same money twice. The revision removed that. **Built on 2026-09-27** ([ADR 0009](../decisions/0009-movements-are-entries.md)): all four automatic writers are live, so the double count this row names, a top-up made in the bank app and again by MoneyBud on assignment, can now happen. **What still keeps it from being worse**: every automatic move is a visible row in both accounts' histories, naming its category, so a double count can be found and a balance correction puts the balance right | **Decided on 2026-09-27, before accounts were built, as this row asked, and built the same day** ([ADR 0008](../decisions/0008-balance-is-worked-out.md)). **No balance is stored anywhere**, in memory or in the file, and a cache of one was rejected as a second author inside MoneyBud. A balance is **derived** from the incomes, expenses and transfers on the account, never stored as a free number. A hand edit is an **adjustment**, a *balance correction*, not an overwrite, and entered and calculated balances are **not** shown side by side. A balance correction is a **dated assertion**: the balance the bank showed on the day it was typed, so every entry dated before it is already in it and only later entries move the balance ([§12](12-glossary.md), *A typed balance is what the bank said that day*). An account with no typed balance, like a first start's *Betaalrekening*, is the plain sum of its entries until first corrected. **What that leaves.** (1) **The double count is not prevented, only made repairable.** Once backing moves money by itself, a top-up also made in the bank app is still counted twice. A balance correction then puts the balance right, and its difference shows in the account's history, but *Accumulated* does not follow it, as the Impact column says. How the two halves meet is for the backing increment. [§12](12-glossary.md) files a related question there: what purpose, if any, a starting balance or balance correction carries (*A question for the backing increment*). **Answered on 2026-09-27: none.** Such money stays seen by location only, and *Accumulated* does not include it (*Money the user already had stays by location only*). So the halves are ruled to differ by it rather than made to meet. (2) **"One entry to re-point" is weaker than this row hoped.** Changing or re-pointing an entry dated before an account's latest balance correction does not change that account's balance, because the balance correction already took it in. So a mis-defaulted expense the user has corrected past no longer shows up in that account's balance, and re-pointing it moves only the account it goes to. In [§12](12-glossary.md)'s reading that is usually the right figure, since the corrected balance is the bank's. (3) **A transfer can change net worth** when one of its two accounts has a balance correction after its date and the other has not. **Ruled true, not a flaw** (2026-09-27): each account follows its own balance corrections, and net worth is their sum. Making the account easy to change after the fact still matters more than any warning at entry would: the account field is a list, pre-filled and visible, and a row names its account when that is not the pool account. That shows a cash expense recorded on the cash account. It does not show one left on the pool by mistake. **For the backing increment's plan** (rulings settled 2026-09-27): ADR 0008 expected backing to "write entries, not balances". The rulings make that harder than it sounds. **A movement for a later period has no destination until its day**, because it goes to whatever the category is backed by on that period's first day ([§12](12-glossary.md), *Planned money follows the backing on the day it moves*). So it cannot be written as a finished entry when it is assigned, unless the entry is rewritten whenever the backing changes first. The revision of unbacking adds moves whose amount is what is there for the category in the backing account on that day, itself a worked-out figure and a second one beside *Accumulated*, so they too must either be worked out or fixed when made. A follow-up fixes the amount moved at backing against later-recorded expenses, and §12 derives the same for unbacking and re-pointing, so all three are amounts fixed on their day while planned movements are not. The alternatives the documentation can see are to work movements out from the assignments and a dated record of each category's backing, which keeps "worked out, never stored", or to write each one on its day, which would be the first thing MoneyBud does *at* a period boundary rather than working out on request. Either is costly to reverse once data is real, and it touches the file format, so it is likely to need a record of its own at the plan gate. **Answered at the backing increment's plan gate, 2026-09-27, and built** ([ADR 0009](../decisions/0009-movements-are-entries.md)): the second alternative. **Every movement is a stored entry, written on the day the money moves**, and money planned for a later period is written by **settling**, the first time MoneyBud runs on or after that period's first day, before anything else is done, with the backing and pool account of that moment. So the automatic writers write entries, as ADR 0008 expected, and a balance is still their sum. Working movements out was rejected because the amounts fixed on their day would have had to be stored anyway. **What that leaves.** (4) **Settling trusts the clock.** Nothing can change while MoneyBud is closed, and that is what lets a late settling see the first day's backing. A clock turned back settles nothing and forgets nothing. **A clock set wrongly ahead**, though, would settle the periods up to that date with that day's backing and move the settled-through day forward for good. The movements would be dated in the future, so balances would not show them until their dates, but they would not follow a backing changed in between. Worse, once the clock is put right, **money assigned afterwards to a backed category in those periods would never move**, and *Opgebouwd* would not count it as planned, because those periods count as settled already. Low: it needs a wrong system date, and a movement that is missing is visible as a balance that did not change. Not guarded: the stakeholder has not been asked, and a guard would have to guess which clock is right. MoneyBud has no repair for it short of starting over; editing the file by hand would also have to remove the movements written ahead (5) **A refused act straight after a period began leaves the settled money unsaved** until the next change, because only an act that changes the ledger saves. Nothing is lost: the next start settles the kept data the same way, with the same result ([§6](06-runtime-view.md), *Settling*). Accepted as a build choice ([§12](12-glossary.md), *Backing: chosen in the build*). **Closed for sweeps only** (ruled at the sweep's scenario stage, 2026-09-27, **built 2026-09-28**, [ADR 0010](../decisions/0010-sweeps-and-period-ends.md)): a sweep that moved money is saved straight away, because it is announced, and settling it again after a restart would announce it twice. **And for occurrences of a recurring entry** since the recurring-entries increment, for the same reason ([ADR 0011](../decisions/0011-recurring-entries.md)). Planned-money moves keep this gap. **The sweep's rulings (settled 2026-09-27, built 2026-09-28) add two writers of the pool account's balance** ([§12](12-glossary.md), *The sweep and Restant*): the sweep itself, by settling at every period end, and the one-click difference, when the user presses it. Both write movements, so a balance is still a sum and every move is a row in both histories. **What they add to this row.** (6) **The sweep moves a worked-out figure, the *Restant*, out of the pool account, and assumes it is there.** It is exactly what is left of the period on the pool account only when the money went where MoneyBud assumes: income onto the pool account, unbacked expenses off it, backed expenses off their backing accounts. **Where it did not, the pool account and the *Restant* part.** A backed category's expense paid from the pool account, or an income put on another account, leaves the pool holding less than the *Restant*, so the sweep overdraws it, shown *Rood* and never blocked. An unbacked expense put on another account leaves the pool holding more, which stays there with no purpose. The cash default above does **not** add to this: a cash expense left on the pool account is wrong about which account paid, but the sweep then matches the pool's recorded balance, and the error stays where it was, between the pool and the cash account. Nothing reports either difference; the pool account's balance shows it. (7) **The clock set wrongly ahead, (4), reaches further**: settling would sweep a period that has not ended, from figures still being entered. The one-click difference repairs that, since a period's figure is worked out again later, but a period swept early by the automatic sweep counts as swept. Low, for the same reason as (4). **Built on 2026-09-28**, and both writers are live: the sweep at settling and *Restant bijwerken*. What the build adds to (7): settling now also writes a **period-end record**, the categories backed at that moment, and a clock set ahead would write it **for good** with the backing of a day that had not come, so that period's *Restant* would be worked out under it for ever after. MoneyBud has no repair for that short of starting over. (8) **The minute after a boundary is handled.** Until the next tick or act settles, the screen can still show *Restant bijwerken* on a period that settling is about to sweep. `spec-reviewer` found that pressing it then made the ledger throw. `MoneyBudApp.BringSweepUpToDate` now settles first and, if the line no longer offers the button, only says the sweep; `SweepScreenTests` holds it ([§6](06-runtime-view.md), *The sweep at settling*). No other act is affected: each one settles first inside the ledger. (9) **Recurring entries (settled 2026-09-28, not built) make settling a third automatic writer**, of incomes and expenses rather than movements ([§12](12-glossary.md), *Recurring entries*). Two points reach this row. **The clock set wrongly ahead, (4), reaches further again**: it would record every occurrence up to that date, incomes and expenses that have not happened, and they stay once the clock is put right; unlike a movement written ahead, each can be removed by hand, and is not recorded again. **"Settling sees exactly what the day saw" no longer holds by itself**: occurrences dated while MoneyBud was closed are written at the next start, so it holds only if settling writes them in date order with the period ends between them. **Ruled so by the stakeholder the same day** (§12, *Recurring entries*, follow-up 5): settling works through the days in order, so a period's occurrences are recorded before it is swept. **Built on 2026-09-28** ([ADR 0011](../decisions/0011-recurring-entries.md)): settling works event by event, each occurrence is saved straight away, and `Settle` lost its once-a-day early return. What the build adds to (9): an occurrence is **never checked**, because only one whose date has come is recorded, so "a future expense is never recorded" rests on the clock alone. A clock set ahead would record **future-dated expenses**, which recording by hand refuses, and move each repeat's next date past them for good; once the clock is put right the next occurrence waits for that later date. Low, for the same reason as (4). (10) **A change of the period start day moves money too** (built 2026-09-29, [ADR 0012](../decisions/0012-the-calendar-is-a-history.md)). When it ends the current period it passes into the next itself, sweeping the ended period and moving the new period's planned money, **dated the day of the change**, so a balance correction typed earlier that day cannot take them in. When a plan made ahead lands back in the current period, which two changes in one period can do, its backed money moves at once, dated that day. All are ordinary movements, rows in both histories, so nothing new about how a balance is written: one more occasion, and one the user confirms. **The clock set wrongly ahead, (4), reaches it**: settling may then already have moved a plan made ahead, dated its old first day, and `spec-reviewer` found a change moving that money a second time. Fixed, with a unit test: the plan is still re-keyed, but a change moves no money for a plan settling has already passed. Low, for the same reason as (4). The difference shows as *Vrij* on that account, and the user gives it a purpose or not. **The double count is still not prevented**: it now shows as *Vrij* too high on the backing account rather than nowhere. **On the pool account it still shows nowhere**, which has no *Vrij* by ruling, a cost accepted with it. **Revised on 2026-10-04, after the install** ([§12](12-glossary.md), *Vrij on the pool account: ruled after the install*): the pool account shows *Vrij* too, its balance less the current period's *Niet toegewezen*, the unbacked *Resterend* and what its backed categories claim, so a difference there now shows as well, and the cost is gone (built 2026-10-05). Backing, "—" and re-pointing now also move an overspending or a shortfall the other way. Each is a row in both histories when it moves money between accounts, and each may overdraw the account it leaves, *Rood*, never blocked. The lock is built from the period a category got its account, so a new expense against a backed category can no longer be put on another account |

| ***Vrij* is a remainder, so it absorbs whatever else is wrong.** Settled 2026-10-04, not built ([§12](12-glossary.md), *Vrij, and moving Opgebouwd*). *Vrij* is an account's balance minus what its categories claim, worked out, so **every error that reaches a non-pool account's balance lands in it**: an expense on the wrong account, a transfer forgotten or entered twice, a top-up counted by MoneyBud and by hand, a balance correction typed with a slip.
