# 11. Risks and Technical Debt

**What belongs here:** Known technical risks and accumulated debt, stated plainly, ideally with
what could be done about each.

This section is only worth anything if it is honest. A risk section listing no real risks tells the
reader the document isn't maintained.

---

| Risk / debt | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Manual entry and accurate net worth pull against each other.** An accurate net worth requires every transaction to be recorded; requiring that is exactly the friction that makes people abandon budgeting apps. The app cannot detect a forgotten transaction, because nothing outside it knows what the truth is | High — this is the most likely cause of the product quietly becoming useless | Not solved. Options include letting balances be corrected by hand, or reconciling entered balances against calculated ones and reporting the difference. Bank import would largely remove it, but conflicts with local operation |
| **Two mechanisms will write the same account balance.** Once accounts exist, an *account-backed* category ([§12](12-glossary.md)) makes a balance move as a **side effect of a planning action**: assigning to Savings transfers real money into its backing account, and the end-of-period sweep does the same unprompted. Both movements now have a designated **source** as well — the *pool account* ([§12](12-glossary.md)) — so one everyday account is written automatically by every assignment to a backed category and by every sweep, which makes it the single balance most exposed to this. Meanwhile round 2 settles that the user can also edit a balance freely by hand. So a balance is authored from two directions — one automatic, one manual — and, as the row above records, nothing outside MoneyBud knows which is right. The failure is quiet: the user tops up savings in the bank app, MoneyBud transfers the same money again on assignment, and the balance is double-counted with nothing to contradict it. **A third route reaches a wrong balance from the other side.** An expense whose category is unbacked now *defaults* to the pool account rather than asking ([§12](12-glossary.md)), so an expense paid in **cash** and entered without overriding the account is deducted from the wrong place: the pool account reads low by money that never left it and the cash account reads high by money that did, and nothing outside MoneyBud will say so. Cash is the likely case precisely because nothing about it looks different at entry. This one is **known and accepted** — it was argued out before the default was taken, the override exists, and the decision saved on every expense was judged worth it ([§12](12-glossary.md), quality goal 2) — so it belongs here as a recognised cost, not as an unnoticed hole | Medium, and **latent** — there are still no accounts in the code, so nothing can go wrong yet. Becomes high the moment backing is built, because a wrong balance corrupts net worth, which is one of the two questions the app exists to answer ([§1.1](01-introduction-and-goals.md)). A hand edit also makes the two halves of the model disagree openly: *Accumulated* ([§12](12-glossary.md)) is computed purely from assignments and expenses, so it does not follow an edited backing account, and because backing is many-to-many the difference cannot be attributed to any one category | Not solved, and deliberately not solved now. Decide it **before** accounts are built rather than after: this is exactly the kind of thing that is expensive to discover late, because the choice shapes how balances are stored. The options to weigh are whether a hand-edit is an overwrite or an adjustment entry, whether a balance is stored or derived from its transactions, and whether MoneyBud shows entered and calculated balances side by side rather than picking one. The expense default adds no separate mitigation and needs none: if balances end up **derived from their transactions**, a mis-defaulted account is one entry to re-point rather than a balance to unpick, which is a point in that option's favour. Making the account easy to change after the fact matters more here than any warning at entry would, since the whole point of the default is that nothing at entry knows it is wrong |
| **Single stakeholder, who is also the only user and only developer.** No external party will notice if the product solves the wrong problem | Medium — a wrong direction can run a long way before anything contradicts it | Stakeholder input is recorded verbatim in `docs/stakeholder/` so that later reasoning can be checked against what was actually said, not against what was remembered |
| **Money representation is decided in code but not on disk** (see [§8.2](08-crosscutting-concepts.md)). Settled: amounts are always a whole number of cents, refused at entry if finer, so nothing in MoneyBud ever rounds; in code an amount is a signed `Money` value type over a `long` of cents, transaction amounts are positive magnitudes with direction carried by the transaction type, and there is no currency field ([ADR 0003](../decisions/0003-money-representation.md)). **What remains is how amounts are stored**, which is open because [§8.3](08-crosscutting-concepts.md) defers persistence as a whole, and **the standing condition on the whole-cents rule**: it holds only while every amount is typed in rather than computed, and §8.2 lists what would reopen it — a split leftover, a percentage, interest, a bank import, a second currency | Low now, and no longer the thing blocking code. Nothing has been written to disk in any increment so far, so there is no stored format to be wrong yet, and `Money` being cents means whatever format is eventually chosen has one obvious thing to write. Rises to medium the moment anything is kept: a stored representation is the part that is genuinely expensive to change once real data exists | The code half is decided; write it. The storage half is deliberately deferred with a stated trigger ([§8.3](08-crosscutting-concepts.md)) — the first time the stakeholder would mind re-entering his data. Treat the standing condition as a live rule rather than history: anyone adding a feature that *computes* an amount reopens §8.2 before writing it, and `Money` has no division in order to make that hard to do by accident |
| **The Desktop has no automated tests, by plan.** `MoneyBud.Desktop` is the Avalonia window and `RingControl`. No scenario and no unit test starts it ([ADR 0006](../decisions/0006-three-source-projects.md)). It was checked by running it on Windows, and by rendering it headless with synthetic data outside the repository ([ADR 0005](../decisions/0005-avalonia-ui-toolkit.md)). That is sound only while it decides nothing. **It did decide one thing, and that was moved out:** as first built, narrowing the suggestions was Avalonia's own *Contains* filter, set in the window's markup. It is now `MoneyBudApp.SuggestionMatches` in the presentation layer, which the Desktop only hands to the control (*Resolved*, below). The one piece of behaviour left in the Desktop is its once-a-minute refresh ([§8.4](08-crosscutting-concepts.md)). **The first demo put three rulings next to that line** (2026-09-26, [§12](12-glossary.md)), and the build kept two of them out of the Desktop. The minimum slice width is `Ring.MinimumSweep` in the presentation layer, and `RingControl`'s own hidden minimum was removed. What pointing shows is decided by `MoneyBudApp` and `Ring.SliceAt`, and the Desktop only turns the pointer's position into a share. The third, the order of a form's fields, can live only in the window's markup. **It is the one exception to "no automated tests"**: `WindowMarkupTests` reads the markup as text, approved at the plan gate as a small departure from ADR 0006 ([§8.4](08-crosscutting-concepts.md), *One test reads the window's markup*) | Low. Everything the screen *decides* is in the presentation layer and is run by the scenarios or unit tests, except the field order, which the markup test holds. What can go wrong here unnoticed is what is *drawn*: a slice in the wrong place, a marker bound to the wrong flag, a button that calls the wrong command. **Compiled bindings** catch a binding to something that does not exist at build time, but not a binding to the wrong thing that does. Rises if logic starts collecting in the window, because that logic would be green by default | Keep "deliberately thin" a **rule**: a decision found in the Desktop moves to `MoneyBud.Presentation`, where the scenarios reach it. Check the window by running it after any change to what the Overview shows. If the drawing ever needs guarding, Avalonia's headless mode is the route to an automated check. It has been used twice outside the repository, the second time to chase the "groc" defect after the first demo ([§12](12-glossary.md)). It was left out deliberately, not overlooked |
| **A settled rule the code cannot honour yet: the period start day cannot change once budgets exist.** [§12](12-glossary.md) says the start day is configurable. `Ledger` takes its `BudgetPeriodCalendar` at construction and has no way to change it, and budgets are stored keyed by category and the **first day** of their period. Changing the start day after any budget exists would leave every budget attached to a period that no longer exists. Expenses and income carry their own dates and would re-sort by themselves; budgets would not. This is why the UI increment keeps the start day at the 1st ([§12](12-glossary.md), *The period start day stays at the 1st, for now*) | Low today. Nothing is kept between runs, so the start day could be chosen before any budget exists without harm. Rises once data is kept, because then there is always a budget in the way | Not solved, and not solvable by code alone. First **decide** what happens to existing budgets when the start day changes, and put it to the stakeholder. Then store or migrate budgets to match. Nothing about it is urgent while the UI does not expose the setting |
| **The demo cannot correct a wrong entry.** The UI increment covers what the domain does, and the domain has no editing or deleting of a transaction ([§12](12-glossary.md), *It covers what the domain does*). A mistyped expense or income stays for the rest of the run, and because nothing is stored ([§8.3](08-crosscutting-concepts.md)), the only way to be rid of it is to close MoneyBud and lose everything else too. That sits against the first half of quality goal 3, amounts adjustable without starting over ([§1.2](01-introduction-and-goals.md)). A plan can be corrected, by assigning; a transaction cannot | Low while the demo's data is throwaway, and a demo is a good place to find out how much it matters. It is also likely to be among the first things the stakeholder reacts to, since a typo is the first mistake anyone makes. **It was:** at the first demo, 2026-09-26, he named deleting entries as missing, and deferred it himself: *"Maar dat komt later"* ([§12](12-glossary.md), *It covers what the domain does*) | **Accepted by the stakeholder for the demo on 2026-09-25**, with the consequence above in front of him. Correcting entries becomes **its own later increment**. It is a domain capability with questions of its own (whether an edit is a new record or a rewrite, what it does to a period already swept), so it goes through stage 1 rather than being added to the UI. **Stands now that the UI is built.** It has already shaped one ruling: "2.000" is refused as ambiguous rather than read as €2,00, partly because a wrong amount, once recorded, cannot be put right ([§12](12-glossary.md), *Typing an amount*). **Settled on 2026-09-26 and planned as the next increment. Not built, so the row stands.** Entries can be changed and removed in any period, removing asks first, categories can be renamed, and a category with no history can be deleted ([§12](12-glossary.md), *An entry can be changed or removed*). Both of this row's questions are answered: a change is a **rewrite**, keeping no record of what the entry was, and a correction reaching a swept period either meets the late-expense rule or joins §12's open question about the sweep. The two points the first write-up left open were settled by the stakeholder the same day. The row moves to *Resolved* when the increment is built |
| **Desktop-first serves the eventual usage pattern badly.** The deployment form is now decided — desktop, [ADR 0002](../decisions/0002-desktop-application-first.md) — which closes the previous "undecided" risk and replaces it with this one. Expenses are entered while out of the house, so mobile is where entry actually happens, and entry friction is what abandons budgeting apps (quality goal 2). A desktop app is the wrong shape for that | Medium — low while the first version is a throwaway demo, because the demo exists to be reacted to rather than lived in. Rises as soon as the stakeholder starts using it in earnest. **The UI now exists, and entry through it is on the desktop only**, which is where this risk stops being theoretical | Accepted knowingly, with the reasoning in ADR 0002. Mobile and desktop/mobile sync stay deferred, not rejected ([§3.2](03-context-and-scope.md)). Keeping scenarios declarative (`features/README.md`) is what lets the specification survive being pointed at a different UI. **The UI increment kept the way to mobile open on two more fronts:** the toolkit also targets iOS and Android ([ADR 0005](../decisions/0005-avalonia-ui-toolkit.md)), and everything the screen decides sits in a layer with no toolkit that a mobile head could use ([ADR 0006](../decisions/0006-three-source-projects.md)). Neither settles mobile. Reopen ADR 0002 when the demo stops being a demo |
| **The "location" dimension is still not built** — not in the expense increment and not in the income one — and end-of-period money therefore has nowhere real to go. **No transaction in the demo has an account — not an expense, and not an income.** An expense has a category but no account. An income has neither: it names no category by design ([§12](12-glossary.md)) and no account for this reason, so an income record is an amount, a date and a label and nothing else. That makes the gap wider on the income side than on the expense side. Money is recorded as *arriving* with no statement of where it arrived, and the *pool account* — which [§12](12-glossary.md) defines as the place unassigned money is assumed to live — has nothing to be. The knock-on: both *Leftover* and still-*Unassigned* money are supposed to be swept into a chosen destination category at a period end, and [§12](12-glossary.md) now requires that destination to be **account-backed** — precisely so that swept money really lands somewhere. With no accounts there can be no backed category, so there is no valid destination and the sweep cannot run at all; in practice that money vanishes at the boundary | Low, while the data is throwaway. Would become high if the demo quietly turns into the real thing — directing what is left over somewhere useful is a core capability ([§1.1](01-introduction-and-goals.md)), and an account is what it needs to point at | Accepted deliberately, and the vanishing money accepted explicitly by the stakeholder on the grounds that the first version is a demo to react to rather than live in ([§1.1](01-introduction-and-goals.md)) and its data is disposable. It stops being acceptable the moment accounts arrive. Revisit then, and in any case before any data is kept that the stakeholder would mind losing. **Income, now built, widened this gap without reopening the decision**: the accountless shape was accepted for the demo as a whole rather than for expenses specifically, and it is the same trade on both sides — an account on an income would need a pool account to be meaningful, and that needs the whole location dimension |

## Resolved

A row leaves the table when its risk is gone. It is listed here so that a reader who remembers it
can see what happened to it.

| Was | Resolved by |
|---|---|
| **The "shown in a period" scenarios were bound to the ledger, not to anything that shows.** The archive scenarios and two assign scenarios asserted that a category "should (not) be shown" in a period, and the steps combined `HasHistoryIn` and `IsArchived`. The archived half of the display rule lived only in the step definitions, and the full rule was settled but implemented nowhere. A green suite could have gone on describing code the user never sees | **The UI increment, 2026-09-25.** The full rule is one domain query, `Ledger.CategoriesShownIn`, and the Overview lists exactly what it returns. The "shown" steps, and "shown as over budget", "shown as over-assigned" and "offered", were rebound to read the presentation layer ([§8.1](08-crosscutting-concepts.md), [§8.4](08-crosscutting-concepts.md)). The in-use half has scenarios of its own in `show-categories-in-a-period.feature` |
| **"2.000" was recorded as €2,00 without a word.** Found by `spec-reviewer` in the UI increment, and resolved before it was ever written into this table. With a point or comma as the decimal mark, "2.000" read as two euros. That is whole cents, so no rule downstream could catch it, while the user very likely meant two thousand. MoneyBud itself shows thousands with a point. And a recorded entry cannot be corrected (above) | **Ruled by the stakeholder on 2026-09-25, after the review, and built.** A mark followed by exactly three digits ending in 0 is refused as ambiguous, and the refusal names both readings ([§12](12-glossary.md), *Typing an amount*). Held by scenarios since 2026-09-26 (next row) |
| **Reading a typed amount had no scenario.** It was ruled at the plan gate and refined after the spec review ([§12](12-glossary.md), *Typing an amount*): comma or point as the decimal mark, no thousands separator, "2.000" refused as ambiguous. It was held only by unit tests (`AmountInputTests`, `FormTests`), and ADR 0004's rule for unit tests says a behaviour asserted only by a unit test means a scenario is missing. A green suite did not show the stakeholder that his ruling held | **`features/type-an-amount.feature`, approved at the scenario gate on 2026-09-26, bound and green**: 14 scenario outlines, 73 cases, across expenses, incomes and assigning. Writing it surfaced two more rulings, taken the same day: four or more whole-cent digits after the mark ("2.0000") are not an amount, and a mark needs a digit on both sides (",50" and "12," are not amounts). Six choices the scenario writer made were approved with it ([§12](12-glossary.md), *Typing an amount*). One of them, that "€ −50" is refused, has no scenario and is held by a unit test instead |
| **Narrowing the suggestions lived in the Desktop, held by nothing.** As first built, the category box narrowed with Avalonia's own `AutoCompleteBox` *Contains* filter, set in the window's markup. That put a stakeholder ruling ([§12](12-glossary.md), *Category entry is free text with suggestions*) where no scenario or test reaches, compared by the thread's culture, and able to regress with every test green after a toolkit upgrade | **Moved into the presentation layer on 2026-09-25.** `MoneyBudApp.SuggestionMatches` and `SuggestionsFor` decide it, ordinally with case ignored and runs of whitespace counted as one, and `FormTests` holds it. The Desktop only passes the predicate to the control as a custom filter. The stakeholder did not ask for a scenario for it. This is the first time the Desktop's "decides nothing" rule (the table above) was enforced |
